# Style Guide

## Tone

Direct and results-oriented by default — leads with a number or a claim, not a hedge. Shifts urgent when a platform change (Apple, iOS) is the subject: opens with an alert emoji and frames it as something the reader needs to act on now, not just know about.

## Sentence structure

Short, punchy declaratives, often stacked as fragments for emphasis ("No spam. No fluff. Just scalable activation and retention."). Uses a rhetorical question to set up the real point ("But with that freedom comes a challenge: How do you actually convert and retain users…"). Longer sentences are reserved for the technical/mechanism explanation in the middle of a post; open and close stay short.

## Vocabulary

Reaches for: growth, retention, activation, funnel, deliverability, engagement, revenue lift, scalable, no-code/low-code. Quantifies everything — percentages and multipliers (5–10% extra revenue, 2x faster, 4x cheaper, up to 30% incremental lift) over vague superlatives. Avoids soft marketing words like "amazing," "revolutionary," "game-changing" — prefers a specific number to a big adjective.

## Punctuation & formatting

Opens key posts with a signal emoji (🚀 for opportunity, 🚨 for a platform-risk alert) as a pattern-interrupt. Uses hashtag clusters at the end of posts (#MobileGrowth #UserEngagement #PushNotifications etc.) rather than inline. Comfortable with a soft CTA that folds into a product plug ("That's where we come in" / "Curious? Let's talk: [link]"). Not lowercase-stylized; standard capitalization and punctuation throughout — the informality lives in sentence rhythm, not in typography.

## Anti-patterns

- Never hedges with "it could be argued" or "some might say" — states the take and moves on.
- Never says "As an AI" or writes in generic corporate-neutral voice — even the product pitch stays specific and numbers-driven.
- Never treats a platform change (App Store policy, iOS update) as neutral news — always reframes it as a strategic implication for app developers within the same breath.
- Wouldn't write a long, unstructured paragraph — breaks momentum with short fragments and CTAs instead of building one dense block of text.
